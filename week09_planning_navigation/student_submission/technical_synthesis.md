Across all three missions, I learned that finding a path is only one part of successful robot navigation. In Mission 1, we compared different search algorithms and saw how some were better at finding shorter paths or searching more efficiently than others. 

With that being said, Mission 2 showed us that even if a robot is able to find a path, it doesn't always mean it can successfully follow it. For example, on the constrained map, A* reached the goal in 90.0 seconds, while DFS failed after 144.6 seconds because of a controller error. DFS had a lot of unnecessary turns and therefore, the route was a lot harder to follow.

Mission 3 showed how important it is to consider people when planning routes. For example, A* with social_2 increased the minimum distance from the person from 0.706 to 0.759 meters without adding a lot of time. However, RRT already stayed far away from the person and adding repulsion didn't improve its results. Overall, I think a good route should be efficient, easy for the robot to follow, and safe for people around it. We also need to keep in mind that the robot's position and distance measurements are estimates meaning they might not be fully accurate.
