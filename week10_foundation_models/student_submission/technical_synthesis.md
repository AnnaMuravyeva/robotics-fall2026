Throughout these three missions, I learned that getting a useful response from an AI model depends not only on how we write our prompts, but also on whether the model understands the robot's capabilities and can correctly use the information given to it. In Mission 1, the original responses showed that the model could make assumptions about the cup or fail to ask for clarification when the instructs were unclear or incomplete.  After I revised the prompt to specify the robot's starting position, destination, and limitations, the response became more realistic. The model focused on navigating to the shelf and inspecting the cup without making any assumptions about it. 

In Mission 2, the model originally suggested using a measuring tape, even though there wasn't one in the photograph. Once I explained that the robot needed to draw a circle, it correctly identified the compass. However, it still failed to give me visual evidence that suggests the object in the photograph is a compass. This mission showed me how important it is to provide enough context for the model so that it doesn't start guessing. 

Mission 3 was probably the most interesting because even with very specific instructions, the robot still couldn't reach its goal. It successfully moved from (1,7) to (1,1), but then it kept trying to move UP into the wall or choosing STOP even after I explicitly said not to use STOP until we reach the goal. The model struggled to figure out when to change directions. The simulator prevented the robot from moving through the wall, but the model still kept repeating the same actions.

Overall, I learned that better prompts can improve the model's response, but they don't guarantee successful robot behavior. One remaining limitation is that the model can still struggle to follow instructions and adjust its actions based on feedback. Even when an AI model receives the correct information, it might not use it correctly to make decisions.
